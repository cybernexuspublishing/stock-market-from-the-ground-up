Social post 1 — What does owning a share mean?

A share represents an ownership interest—not a promise of profit. Before reading stock charts, learn the difference between owning a share, seeing a quote, and submitting an order. Our beginner path starts there. Read the free five-day overview: https://stock-market-from-the-ground-up-d1r.pages.dev/free-foundations-challenge.html

Social post 2 — Last trade is not your fill price

A chart displays a last trade at $20.00. Does that guarantee your next market buy fills at $20.00? No. Bid, ask, available size, and changing prices matter. Learn the distinction before practicing with real money. https://stock-market-from-the-ground-up-d1r.pages.dev/how-stock-market-works.html

Social post 3 — A useful reason to begin free

New to stocks? Try one fictional chart exercise, read the explanation, and decide what the example actually proves. No login is needed for the practice page. Nothing here is a trading signal. https://stock-market-from-the-ground-up-d1r.pages.dev/candle-practice.html

Social post 4 — Four prices make a candle

Open, high, low, close: four values from one interval. One candle cannot tell you which extreme happened first or predict the next bar. Try three free, fictional candlestick questions: https://stock-market-from-the-ground-up-d1r.pages.dev/candle-practice.html

Social post 5 — A wick isn't a forecast

A long upper wick means price traded above the body during the interval. It does not prove who traded, why the move happened, or whether the next candle must fall. Read the values before the story. https://stock-market-from-the-ground-up-d1r.pages.dev/how-to-read-candlestick-charts.html

Social post 6 — Start a five-day study habit

Our five Free Foundations topics are shares and quotes, candlesticks, source claims, research watchlists, and risk/no-trade decisions. Study at your own pace; no consecutive-day streak is required. Explore the curriculum: https://stock-market-from-the-ground-up-d1r.pages.dev/free-foundations-challenge.html

Social post 7 — A screenshot is not a source

A stock screenshot can omit the timestamp, quotation type, and what happened before the clip. Before treating a post as evidence, find the primary release and the time it became public. This is what our third Free Foundations lesson teaches. https://stock-market-from-the-ground-up-d1r.pages.dev/free-foundations-challenge.html

Social post 8 — Your watchlist is not a buy list

A research watchlist should say why each candidate matters, where the claim came from, when it was checked, and what would cause you to remove it. “No Trade” is a valid decision. https://stock-market-from-the-ground-up-d1r.pages.dev/free-foundations-challenge.html

Social post 9 — Market orders and limit orders

A market order prioritizes execution; a limit order controls your acceptable price but may not fill. Neither eliminates risk. Our free explanation uses a fictional quote, not real prices or a recommendation. https://stock-market-from-the-ground-up-d1r.pages.dev/market-vs-limit-orders.html

Social post 10 — Risk math is not a guarantee

A fictional trade has a planned entry and a stop-reference distance. Dividing a risk budget by that distance gives a modeled share count—not a guarantee your stop will execute there. Learn the limitations first. https://stock-market-from-the-ground-up-d1r.pages.dev/stock-trading-risk-management.html

Social post 11 — Support isn't a wall

Past reactions near a price range can make a useful study reference, but that level can fail. Do not move it afterward just to fit the outcome. This free guide explains the difference. https://stock-market-from-the-ground-up-d1r.pages.dev/support-resistance-basics.html

Social post 12 — A good decision may be no trade

Can you explain what the evidence supports, what remains unknown, and what would invalidate your idea? If not, the disciplined choice might be to observe. Free Foundations is built to practice that reasoning before paid tools matter. https://stock-market-from-the-ground-up-d1r.pages.dev/free-foundations-challenge.html

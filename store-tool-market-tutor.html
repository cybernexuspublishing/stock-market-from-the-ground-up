What Market Tutor actually teaches

Market Tutor is an educational guide for stock-market terminology, chart-reading methods, evidence checks and the member's own practice process. It helps explain concepts and possible mistakes; it does not monitor a brokerage account or direct you to buy or sell a security. Its responses must not be represented as a verified trading outcome, trade recommendation or professional certification.

Worked example — what is knowable from a candle?

Fictional lesson prompt: “A daily candle is still forming at 10:00 a.m. Can I treat its eventual completed high as information available now?”

Teaching explanation: No. At 10:00, the current daily high is provisional; later trading can change it. Record the source, observation timestamp, session, timeframe and values actually seen. A completed day's high shown on a later screenshot is not evidence that it was knowable earlier.

Try a related misconception

“If a candle makes a new high after my decision, that proves my earlier prediction was correct.” Not necessarily. Using that later price to justify an earlier decision introduces hindsight. Compare the original written decision with only the evidence available at that time, then review later observations separately. This is an original fictional teaching example, not authenticated market history.

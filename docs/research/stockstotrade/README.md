## Source methodology
1. Discover URLs via the site's navigation, published sitemap locations where accessible, internal links, and indexed search. Record discovered candidates separately from reviewed pages; avoid asserting that a sample is exhaustive.
2. For each article, record canonical URL, title, author when available, first publication date (and separately any update date), exact educational claim in original researcher paraphrase, and context. Do not assume a search-result publication date is verified if not visible on the page.
3. Split factual regulatory claims, marketing assertions, personal trading approaches, and educational definitions. Check regulatory requirements against SEC, FINRA, exchange rules, and applicable broker documentation, with effective dates and transition rules.
4. For YouTube, maintain video ID, public title, upload date, description, transcript accessibility, and timestamped findings *only when the actual transcript is accessed*. Never label description-based research as a transcript review.
5. Map findings to **existing** programs and the canonical 419-Lab registry only after confirming individual Lab IDs. Do not invent new official competencies.
6. Write original examples, fresh fictional case data, and independent explanations. Avoid storing copyrighted full text/transcripts or republishing provider graphics without permission.
7. Mark each source as discovered, partial, reviewed, authoritative-checked, or deferred. Preserve audit history and uncertainty. Treat trading profit predictions as unverified absent appropriate evidence.
8. No live trading, paid APIs, commercial subscriptions, payments, production content deployment, or new branches.

## Immediate findings and source distinctions
- The 2020 afternoon VWAP/HOD article discusses a discretionary afternoon strategy and author-specific thresholds (float below 20m shares, preferably 10m; at least twice a 60-day volume benchmark). These are **not independently established winning criteria**. Keep detailed pattern practice in BC-45-CHART; source verification belongs in BC-05-TRUTH.
- The 2020 catalyst articles teach earnings, contracts, product news, clinical announcements, and social attention as possible catalysts. Each real case needs timestamped issuer/exchange/regulator evidence.
- SEC primary source for Form 8-K due-date instruction: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/form-8-k — generally four business days after triggering events, with exceptions; avoid teaching that an 8-K must precede all announcements.
- Date-sensitive pattern-day-trading or brokerage margin claims require a newly dated review before inclusion in member content.
- Financial content requires disclosure of simulation assumptions and must not imply guaranteed returns.

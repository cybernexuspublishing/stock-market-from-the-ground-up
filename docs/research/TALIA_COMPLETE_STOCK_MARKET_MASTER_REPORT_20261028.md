### 1. Structural chart reading (`CHART_STRUCTURE`)

A trend is a sequence of observable swing relationships, not a guarantee that the next swing will continue.

**How taught:** Mark two consecutive observable swing highs and lows; explain whether each exceeds or undercuts the preceding swing.

**Limitations:** A breakout over a level is a hypothesis about continuation, not proof of buying intent.

**What invalidates it:** The next observed swing invalidates the proposed structure, or the chart lacks enough visible pivots.

### 2. Candles and price information (`CANDLE_READING`)

A candle summarizes OHLC observations for one interval; it does not encode the sequence between the high and low.

**How taught:** Identify body, upper/lower wicks, open, close and total range on a completed candle.

**Limitations:** A large upper wick can be consistent with rejection, but cannot prove who sold or why.

**What invalidates it:** A wick interpretation conflicts with the broader visible series or an unseen candle is required.

**How taught:** Describe where the currently displayed price is relative to the properly scoped VWAP.

**What invalidates it:** The comparison depends on missing trade/volume inputs or a different session boundary.

## IV. C — Existing 32 STT original research guides (retained)



### 1. How to read SEC filings (`SEC_FILINGS`)

Public-company reporting includes periodic financial statements and event disclosures; the filing itself should be read before treating a social-media summary as evidence.

**How taught:** Identify the issuer's exact legal name and ticker at the event date, locate its SEC filing, record form type, acceptance timestamp, reporting period and the specific disclosed fact.

**Limitations:** A press release, filing headline or missing document is not proof of price impact, profitability or market consensus.

**What invalidates it:** The underlying filing is unavailable, refers to another issuer or period, or was accepted after the replay pause point.

**Self-check:** Find the filing type, exact timestamp and the claim that can be checked independently.

---

### 2. Ticker symbols, security identity and share classes (`ISSUER_IDENTITY`)

A displayed ticker is not a permanent security identifier; corporate actions, share classes and reuse of symbols can make historical comparisons misleading.

**How taught:** Check issuer legal name, exchange or OTC venue, class of security, the relevant date, and whether corporate actions changed the identity before combining records.

**Limitations:** Matching symbols do not establish the same security or identical outstanding share rights.

**What invalidates it:** A later issuer, class or reverse split is silently treated as interchangeable with a prior ticker-day record.

**Self-check:** Which evidence shows these two ticker-day observations refer to the same traded security?

---

### 3. Float, shares outstanding and dilution (`FLOAT_OUTSTANDING`)

Float is the portion of shares generally available for public trading, while outstanding shares include all issued shares held by investors and insiders; counts can change.

**How taught:** State the effective date, filing source, share class, restricted holdings and any split, offering or conversion before comparing float claims.

**Limitations:** A reported float or outstanding count from an old page does not establish today's freely tradable supply or future price direction.

**What invalidates it:** An offering, conversion, restriction change or split occurs after the referenced share count.

**Self-check:** What would you verify before claiming a stock has a particular float after an offering?

---

### 4. Reverse splits and corporate actions (`CORPORATE_ACTIONS`)

Reverse splits, splits, dividends and symbol changes can alter the scale or meaning of historical prices, shares and chart patterns without creating an equivalent change in economic value.

**How taught:** Check record/effective dates and adjustment conventions; compare raw and split-adjusted OHLCV only with the adjustment method clearly disclosed.

**Limitations:** A mechanically adjusted past chart is not an unmodified tape of actual historical trade prices.

**What invalidates it:** The displayed chart or float mix uses one adjustment basis while the comparison uses another.

**Self-check:** How would a reverse split change a chart comparison if its adjustment basis is omitted?

---

### 5. Regular and extended-hours trading sessions (`MARKET_SESSIONS`)

Premarket, regular hours and after-hours sessions may have different participation, liquidity, auction mechanisms and order availability.

**How taught:** Label the exchange time zone and session boundary, then check whether a scanner, candle, volume total, VWAP anchor or order permits extended-hours prints.

**Limitations:** An extended-hours crossing cannot automatically satisfy a regular-session trigger defined for a later time.

**What invalidates it:** A bar, scanner signal or VWAP value silently switches session scope.

**Self-check:** If a plan requires a 09:45 regular-session break, does an 08:50 premarket crossing qualify?

---

### 6. NBBO, consolidated quotations and market-data scope (`NBBO_SIP`)

The National Best Bid and Offer (NBBO) summarizes protected best-priced quotes within its applicable scope; exchange feeds and SIP/consolidated data can differ in coverage and timing.

**How taught:** Identify which venues, quote condition, time stamps, reporting source, odd-lot treatment and vendor entitlements the screen actually uses.

**Limitations:** The NBBO is not the entire order book and a displayed quote is not a guaranteed fill.

**What invalidates it:** Two displays use different feeds or data-latency assumptions and are interpreted as the same executable snapshot.

**Self-check:** What feed and timestamp evidence would be needed to compare two quoted spreads fairly?

---

### 7. Displayed Level II depth versus real liquidity (`DISPLAYED_DEPTH`)

Level II displays visible quoted interest for the venues and participants supplied by the data service; hidden orders, cancellations and order routing limit what it can prove.

**How taught:** Record source and timestamp, compare bid/ask levels and sizes, and check actual prints separately from quotes when making an order-flow claim.

**Limitations:** Displayed size can disappear or be exhausted and cannot guarantee fill quantity or reveal every participant's intent.

**What invalidates it:** Quote data and matched executions disagree, or the displayed book is incomplete or delayed.

**Self-check:** How would you distinguish an advertised 10,000-share bid from 10,000 executed shares?

---

### 8. Time & Sales prints and aggressor inference (`TAPE_EVIDENCE`)

A trade print records an executed price, quantity and time in the feed; quote-relative color conventions are platform-specific and may not fully identify aggressor or participant motives.

**How taught:** Verify trade and quote timestamp alignment, correct reporting conditions, venue coverage and the platform's red/green/white labeling before interpreting a tape sequence.

**Limitations:** One print or color alone does not establish institutional activity or a reliable directional forecast.

**What invalidates it:** The quote was stale, the print is corrected, or feed ordering prevents a sound classification.

**Self-check:** What additional data would you need before classifying a sequence as buyer-initiated?

---

### 9. Bid-ask spread, slippage and execution costs (`SPREAD_SLIPPAGE`)

The spread is the difference between displayed offer and bid; slippage, fees, market impact and partial fills can make realized execution costs larger than a simple model.

**How taught:** Identify quote freshness, expected executable side, displayed depth, order size, actual fills, commissions and the time/session before calculating costs.

**Limitations:** Last-traded price, midpoint and stop trigger are not guaranteed execution prices.

**What invalidates it:** Liquidity vanishes, spreads widen or the actual order fills outside the planned assumptions.

**Self-check:** Which price would a hypothetical market buy most likely interact with first, and what remains uncertain?

---

### 10. Orders, acknowledgments, partial fills and cancellation (`ORDER_LIFECYCLE`)

An order can be submitted, accepted, partially filled, pending cancel, canceled, rejected or fully executed; a cancel request is not itself a confirmed cancel.

**How taught:** Track broker acknowledgment, execution reports, remaining quantity, time in force and final order status before describing exposure or a completed trade.

**Limitations:** A chart touch or click does not establish that an entire order filled at the requested price.

**What invalidates it:** Execution messages show a partial fill or the requested cancellation has not been acknowledged.

**Self-check:** What position might remain after an order for 200 shares fills 75 and the cancel is pending?

---

### 11. IOC, FOK, Day and good-til-canceled instructions (`ORDER_TIME_IN_FORCE`)

Time-in-force rules govern how long an order may seek execution: IOC can partly execute immediately, FOK requires an immediate full execution, and Day/GTC duration depends on order and broker rules.

**How taught:** Check exact broker support, market session, quantity conditions, cancel status and whether partial execution is permitted before comparing instructions.

**Limitations:** These are conditions on orders, not assurances that any desired price or full quantity is available.

**What invalidates it:** The assumed broker does not support the instruction or its actual policy differs from the general label.

**Self-check:** Would IOC and FOK handle a 100-share order differently when only 40 are available immediately?

---

### 12. Stop orders, stop-limit orders and gap risk (`STOP_ORDER_MECHANICS`)

A stop price is a trigger, not a guaranteed exit price: an ordinary stop typically becomes a market order, whereas a stop-limit can remain unfilled.

**How taught:** Separate trigger, resulting order type, acceptable limit, possible gap, available quantity and the broker's trigger convention before estimating an exit.

**Limitations:** A modeled stop loss does not cap real loss under gaps, halts or thin liquidity.

**What invalidates it:** Trading gaps past the trigger or a limit has no available counterparty at the required price.

**Self-check:** How do triggered stop-market and stop-limit orders differ after a large price gap?

---

### 13. Volatility trading pauses, halts and suspensions (`HALTS_LULD`)

Limit Up-Limit Down pauses, exchange news or regulatory halts, and SEC trading suspensions have different causes, scopes and reopening rules.

**How taught:** Identify the specific authority, halt code, trigger time, covered instrument, market data and resumption conditions before discussing available execution.

**Limitations:** A pause does not promise that a position can be exited at the pre-halt price or at a particular minute.

**What invalidates it:** The trading venue has not reopened or the reopening auction price and liquidity are unknown.

**Self-check:** How would you verify whether a halt is a volatility pause versus a regulatory suspension?

---

### 14. Short-sale price-test restriction and Regulation SHO (`SSR_SHO`)

The Rule 201 short-sale circuit breaker generally follows a 10 percent intraday price decline and imposes a price-test restriction for the remainder of the day and the next trading day, subject to exceptions.

**How taught:** Verify the security, trigger reference, restriction status and broker locate requirements rather than assuming all short sales are prohibited.

**Limitations:** SSR does not itself prevent every short sale or prove that a squeeze must occur.

**What invalidates it:** Restriction or locate availability is not confirmed for the actual security and session.

**Self-check:** What does SSR restrict, and what additional borrow or locate checks are still needed?

---

### 15. Short borrow, recall and asymmetrical short risk (`SHORT_BORROW`)

Selling stock short creates obligations related to borrowed shares and may expose the trader to borrowing charges, recalls, dividends and theoretically unbounded price-loss potential.

**How taught:** Identify locate and borrow status, broker margin terms, current fee estimate, dividend dates, position size and the contingency for a halt or gap.

**Limitations:** A borrow indication or modeled stop does not guarantee availability, execution or a maximum loss.

**What invalidates it:** Shares become hard to borrow, borrow is recalled or the price gaps beyond the risk model.

**Self-check:** Which costs and failure scenarios make a short position different from simply owning shares?

### 16. Trade date, T+1 settlement and available funds (`SETTLEMENT_T1`)

Most applicable U.S. securities transactions moved to standard next-business-day settlement, T+1, on May 28, 2024; trade execution and final cash/securities settlement are distinct events.

**How taught:** Record the trade date, business-day calendar, applicable security and account type, then check the broker's actual settlement and available-funds policy.

**Limitations:** T+1 does not mean instant settlement, identical rules for all instruments or unconditional permission to reuse cash.

**What invalidates it:** The transaction or holiday calendar falls outside the assumed settlement rule.

**Self-check:** If an eligible stock trade occurs Monday with no holidays, what is its standard settlement day?

---

### 17. Clearing, custody and SIPC versus market losses (`CLEARING_SIPC`)

Clearing and settlement transfer obligations after trades. SIPC is a limited customer-protection mechanism for failed member brokerages, not insurance against falling share prices.

**How taught:** Separate order execution, broker custody, clearing, settlement, SIPC-member status and asset coverage before comparing risks.

**Limitations:** SIPC does not reimburse ordinary investment losses or guarantee a trade's future value.

**What invalidates it:** A brokerage or asset is outside applicable SIPC coverage or its specific claim conditions.

**Self-check:** What type of brokerage failure can SIPC address, and what investment loss does it not cover?

---

### 18. Market-data provenance, delays and rights of use (`MARKET_DATA_RIGHTS`)

Chart and quote feeds can be delayed, venue-limited, corrected or subject to licensing; an image or downloaded historical series is not automatically authorized for redistribution.

**How taught:** Record provider, product, timestamp convention, session, corporate-action treatment, venue coverage and redistribution license for every market-data example.

**Limitations:** One vendor's entitlement to display data does not give a third-party publisher permission to resell or embed it.

**What invalidates it:** A dataset cannot be reproduced from its cited feed or its license does not permit the intended use.

**Self-check:** Which five source attributes must be recorded before embedding a historical chart in a paid lesson?

### 19. Scanner discovery versus verified trade eligibility (`SCANNER_STATES`)

A scanner discovers candidates using configured filters; qualification, setup development, trigger confirmation, risk clearance and any order decision are separate states.

**How taught:** Record the scanner version and detection time, confirm security identity and feed, then evaluate catalyst, liquidity, structure, trigger, veto and NO TRADE conditions.

**Limitations:** Neither a vendor alert nor a high scanner score validates a strategy's edge or authorizes automatic entry.

**What invalidates it:** One hard gate fails, a quote is stale or the proposed strategy's entry trigger never occurs.

**Self-check:** Which requirements remain after a ticker first appears on a scanner result list?

### 20. Relative volume and matched-time baselines (`RELATIVE_VOLUME`)

Relative volume compares observed trading activity with a declared historical baseline. Meaningful comparisons require the same elapsed interval, session and source coverage.

**How taught:** Specify cumulative versus per-bar volume, matched time of day, baseline days and excluded sessions before interpreting an RVOL figure.

**Limitations:** A large ratio does not reveal aggressor identity or promise price continuation.

**What invalidates it:** The numerator covers five minutes but the comparison denominator covers an entire day.

**Self-check:** How would you build a fair RVOL comparison at 10:00 a.m. on two different days?

---

### 21. Volume, float turnover and participation limits (`VOLUME_PARTICIPATION`)

Volume is matched share quantity, while turnover is a ratio of traded shares to a stated share count; neither independently identifies unique holders or the number of shares available to buy.

**How taught:** Check the share-count effective date, session/feed scope, and any corrections before comparing cumulative volume with float or outstanding shares.

**Limitations:** A value above 100 percent float turnover does not prove every float share changed hands exactly once.

**What invalidates it:** The float estimate is stale or repeated turnover is treated as unique inventory.

**Self-check:** Can the same share trade more than once in a session, and what does that mean for float rotation?

---

### 22. Multi-timeframe chart reading without lookahead (`MULTITIMEFRAME`)

Daily, five-minute and one-minute candles provide different context and structure. A higher timeframe may define location while a shorter timeframe exposes developing evidence.

**How taught:** Choose the context and trigger timeframes before analysis; use only completed higher-timeframe bars available at the lower-timeframe decision pause.

**Limitations:** An unfinished five-minute candle cannot lend its eventual close to a decision at minute two.

**What invalidates it:** A higher-timeframe bar is finalized by future ticks not yet available at the lower-timeframe pause.

**Self-check:** What did the five-minute bar actually show when the two-minute mark had elapsed?

---

### 23. True range, ATR and gaps (`ATR_GAP`)

True range for a bar takes the maximum of high minus low, absolute high minus prior close, and absolute low minus prior close; ATR smooths these magnitudes over a specified lookback.

**How taught:** Use prior close, current high and low, then identify lookback, smoothing method, bar interval and whether price adjustments are consistent.

**Limitations:** ATR measures historical movement magnitude, not up/down direction or a maximum possible future range.

**What invalidates it:** A gap relative to previous close is missing from the true-range inputs.

**Self-check:** If prior close is $10, high $12 and low $11, why is true range $2 rather than $1?

### 24. VWAP session anchors and bar approximations (`VWAP_SCOPE`)

Volume-weighted average price depends on the selected trades or bars, their volume, the calculation's price proxy, and the session anchor.

**How taught:** State the anchor, volume feed, included sessions, and whether transaction prices or typical bar price are weighted before comparing two VWAP plots.

**Limitations:** An identical ticker can have different VWAP readings from different anchors or price approximations.

**What invalidates it:** One view includes extended hours and another starts only at the regular open.

**Self-check:** Why could two correctly computed VWAP lines differ at the same clock time?

### 25. Catalyst verification and event chronology (`CATALYST_CHRONOLOGY`)

A catalyst hypothesis links an issuer event and market attention but must preserve whether the disclosure was actually public by the decision timestamp.

**How taught:** Track issuer identity, primary filing or announcement, posting/acceptance time, timezone, reported versus independently checked claims, and uncertainty.

**Limitations:** A later-released filing or retrospectively reported event is not contemporaneous evidence for an earlier trade decision.

**What invalidates it:** The event source or event-time reconciliation cannot be established.

**Self-check:** How can an earnings announcement released at 16:05 be used for a 15:40 decision?

### 26. Pattern names, trigger variants and no-trade rules (`SETUP_VARIANTS`)

A broad pattern name such as Dip and Rip can describe materially different triggers; first-dip, premarket reclaim and candle-body break are not one universal entry algorithm.

**How taught:** Name the exact variant, observable trigger, timeframe, session, invalidation and any hard veto before reviewing a hypothetical outcome.

**Limitations:** A favorable retrospective example does not prove that the described variant has repeatable positive expectancy.

**What invalidates it:** The required trigger is absent, observed too early or replaced with a different rule after the fact.

**Self-check:** How would you distinguish a candle-body trigger from a wick-high reclaim?

### 27. Predictions, outcomes and evidence quality (`RESEARCH_OUTCOMES`)

A source-reported prediction and a later outcome require a valid time-ordered link. Missing outcome evidence is not automatically a loss or a win.

**How taught:** Freeze the original timestamped prediction and rule, verify security identity and eligible market data, then record whether outcome evidence is linked, absent or contradictory.

**Limitations:** Positive source anecdotes cannot establish statistical win rate, expectancy, prediction calibration or a profitable trading edge.

**What invalidates it:** A prediction is revised after the result or attributed to a different security/date.

**Self-check:** What distinction separates a sourced claimed outcome from independently validated trade evidence?

### 28. Backtesting bias, overfitting and forward-paper evaluation (`BACKTEST_BIAS`)

Looking at future data, survivorship selection, changed rules and repeated testing on the same sample can create convincing-looking but unreliable historical results.

**How taught:** Freeze a strategy specification, timestamp data permissions, segregate development and out-of-sample periods, include realistic costs, and record sample/exclusion decisions.

**Limitations:** A promising backtest or manually selected set of winners does not guarantee future returns or justify promotion without forward evidence.

**What invalidates it:** Any supposedly out-of-sample set influenced parameter selection, or sample construction omitted failures.

**Self-check:** Why does choosing a rule after viewing its test results invalidate a clean out-of-sample claim?

---

### 29. Position sizing, modeled R and gap-adjusted risk (`RISK_SIZING`)

Planned price risk is the distance between entry and technical invalidation times whole-share quantity; actual loss can exceed this model because an executable exit is uncertain.

**How taught:** Choose the invalidation from chart evidence, calculate per-share risk, divide a fixed risk budget, round down, and separately model slippage, gaps, borrow and fees.

**Limitations:** Risk formulas are not live-order permission or guarantees of the maximum possible loss.

**What invalidates it:** Entry, stop or share count cannot be verified, or expected execution loss violates the risk budget.

**Self-check:** Why does 100 shares times a $0.20 planned stop distance not guarantee a $20 maximum actual loss?

---

### 30. Daily risk controls, halts and NO TRADE (`DAILY_CONTINGENCY`)

A risk plan includes exposure limits, correlated positions, operational failures, no-trade vetoes and a process for unexpected halts and uncertain order status.

**How taught:** Define decision and exposure thresholds prospectively, monitor actual filled quantities, and state actions for data outages, rejects, abnormal spreads and halted markets.

**Limitations:** Daily loss targets and soft controls cannot guarantee an exit if trading is paused or an order cannot fill.

**What invalidates it:** Actual exposure or a broker restriction cannot be measured or a hard stop condition is breached.

**Self-check:** What should a learner record if a halt begins while an exit order has a pending-cancel state?

---

### 31. Journal decision versus retrospective review (`JOURNAL_PROVENANCE`)

A useful trading journal separates timestamped pre-outcome evidence and plans from fills, later results and lessons, so hindsight does not rewrite decisions.

**How taught:** Freeze the plan and invalidation at the pause; later attach execution reports, costs, observed outcomes, uncertainty and a separately dated review.

**Limitations:** An attractive screenshot, a hypothetical fill or a self-tagged reflection is not a verified trade or independently assessed competency.

**What invalidates it:** The original decision was edited after future bars appeared, or the fill record has no order reference.

**Self-check:** How would you audit whether a profitable-looking journal entry reflects a decision made before the outcome?

---

### 32. Earnings, valuation and dividends (`FUNDAMENTALS_VALUATION`)

Revenue, earnings, shares outstanding, valuation multiples and dividends describe distinct issuer characteristics; a single P/E or earnings surprise does not establish fair value or a day-trading signal.

**How taught:** Check the issuer's filing period, earnings definition, basic versus diluted share basis, one-time items, capital structure and dividend declaration/ex-dividend dates.

**Limitations:** A low multiple, a dividend headline or a headline EPS figure does not prove safety, growth or a guaranteed return.

**What invalidates it:** The compared companies or periods use noncomparable earnings measures or one-time events dominate reported profit.

**Self-check:** What makes two companies' P/E ratios potentially misleading even if their prices are similar?

### 1. Why enter, hold, exit or wait: the decision framework (`DECISION_FRAMEWORK`)

## VII. F — Historical replay: what exists and what still requires verification



### 1. Replay evidence chronology (`REPLAY_DISCIPLINE`)

Replay is valid only when a learner can inspect evidence available at the recorded pause point. Future candles, later headlines and eventual outcomes remain hidden until the original decision is locked.

**How taught:** Identify the first permitted observation. Record what was visible and what remains uncertain. Commit the decision with its timestamp; only then advance a single chronological step.

**Limitations:** Author-created fictional replay teaches process mechanics, not what occurred for a named historical security. A historical exercise additionally requires independently verified source evidence and redistribution rights.

**What invalidates it:** A later bar is disclosed before commitment, a dataset changes during a published replay, or the original reasoning is changed after the outcome.

**Self-check:** At what time was this decision locked, which observations were visible, and what evidence must still be withheld?



### Historical replay truth standard

1. Verify issuer identity, share class, corporate actions and ticker-date consistency.
2. Obtain complete licensed raw/adjusted OHLCV including feed, session, time zone and bar boundary.
3. Establish contemporaneous filing or news publication timestamps; a later release is not available to an earlier decision.
4. Record candidate scanner discovery versus actual setup qualification and trigger.
5. Pre-map levels using only bars then completed, including HOD/PMH/PDH/VWAP anchor and matched-time volume.
6. Pause the chart, ask the member what is known, unknown and invalidated, and lock their answer before later bars.
7. Distinguish hypothetical stop or market price from executable bid/ask, broker order state, partial fills and halt conditions.
8. Reveal outcome stepwise, preserve original decision, and compare both success and failed/no-trade scenarios.
9. Keep market data license and reuse rights with the educational case and grade only with approved protected Lab rubrics.
10. Never equate an archived claimed profit with actual independently verified execution.

### Advanced watchlist field specification

**Security identity:** issuer legal name, ticker, effective share class, exchange, reverse-split history, float/outstanding effective dates.

### Four suggested independent watchlist lanes

1. **Premarket catalyst and liquidity** — source verified gap, genuine dated news, same-clock participation and actual quote feasibility. It does not authorize an entry during a thin premarket print.
2. **Intraday trend/breakout** — completed resistance, volume/price acceptance, VWAP context and strict no-chase invalidation.
3. **Swing/fundamental research** — daily structure, earnings, valuation, balance sheet, sector comparison and calendar review; do not confuse with one-minute chart scalps.
4. **No-trade and rejected candidates** — dated hard veto and a separate outcome field, so later attractive returns do not rewrite the original reason.

## XI. Original worked calculations and transfer tests

These are **fictional arithmetic examples** and do not represent real historical prices or executed trades.

1. **SMA:** closes $10, $12 and $14 give a three-bar simple average of $12. Different windows and unfinished bars change the output.
2. **EMA:** for length 3, smoothing alpha=2/4=0.5. With a stated $10 seed and successive prices $12, $14 and $12, the EMA values are $11, $12.50 and $12.25. A different seed is a different definition.
3. **Transaction VWAP:** 100 shares at $10, 200 at $10.50 and 100 at $11 produce ($1,000+$2,100+$1,100)/400 = $10.50. A bar-level approximation may differ.
4. **ATR gap:** prior close $10, high $12, low $11: true range is max($1,$2,$1) = $2; high-low alone misses the gap.
5. **Matched-time RVOL:** 900,000 shares traded cumulatively by 10:00 against 300,000 historical average shares at the *same time* implies RVOL 3.0. Full-day denominators are not equivalent.
6. **Position sizing:** entry $10, technical invalidation $9.50, modeled $0.50 price-distance loss and hypothetical budget $75 imply 150 whole shares before fees. The $75 is not a guaranteed maximum realized loss.
7. **R-multiple:** the above hypothetical plan targeting $11 models 2R reward distance against $0.50 initial risk; the calculation says nothing about the probability of reaching $11.
8. **A correct NO TRADE:** a late-released announcement and wide spreads may prevent a qualified entry even when the stock later rallies; mark the reasoning, not the hindsight return.

About The Stock Market: From the Ground Up

The Stock Market: From the Ground Up is a curriculum-first stock-market education series by J. S. Grant. It is designed for readers who want to build market knowledge in prerequisite order rather than jump directly into isolated indicators, patterns, or trading strategies.

About the Author — J. S. Grant

J. S. Grant is a self-taught technologist and certified IT Infrastructure Specialist with a long-standing background in programming, computer repair, and understanding how complex technical systems work. That systems-oriented background shaped the way he approaches market education: break a complicated system into understandable parts, teach the dependencies in the correct order, test the learner against evidence, and require practice before relying on the skill.

Grant created The Stock Market: From the Ground Up to provide a structured path from absolute-beginner market knowledge toward independent market reading and trading decision-making. Rather than treating the market as a collection of shortcuts or disconnected signals, the series emphasizes market mechanics, terminology, evidence, historical context, risk awareness, reproducible examples, and independent reasoning.

His instructional objective is not to tell readers what to buy or sell. It is to teach them how to understand what they are looking at, reproduce the lesson, make a decision using the information available at that moment, and then verify the reasoning afterward.

What the series is designed to do

The Foundations sequence begins with no assumed market knowledge and progressively teaches the structure of the stock market, market participants, orders, price and volume behavior, chart reading, company research, indicators, risk, and the decision-making context needed before more advanced trading material is introduced.

The books are designed to work on their own. The companion learning environment extends the books with searchable Historical Lab IDs, synchronized progress, reader resources, verification records, membership learning tools, and community discussion.

The learning method

The instructional sequence follows seven stages: Learn → Understand → Observe → Reproduce → Decide → Practice → Verify. Concepts are taught before they are tested. Historical exercises identify the market example, date, time, timeframe, and relevant study settings so readers can reproduce the lesson when a compatible historical simulator is available.

No-look-ahead discipline is used where applicable: the learner evaluates the market using only information available at the stated observation point before revealing what happened later. The objective is not to memorize hindsight-perfect charts, but to practice reasoning under the same information constraint that exists in live markets.

Portable learning and historical practice

In-book visuals remain part of the curriculum so readers can study away from a desktop simulator. Conceptual diagrams are used for mechanisms such as exchange structure, order routing, bid/ask mechanics, share issuance, settlement, and market-participant relationships. Lessons about actual market behavior are paired with historical-market examples and reproducible simulator exercises when applicable.

The companion website today

The companion website provides an account-based learning environment built around the books, not a reproduction of their complete text. It brings together the 419 canonical Lab references, guided instructional activities, the Boot Camp Academy, 30 tracked learning competencies, the Trading Journal, research and watchlist workflows, the Market Simulator, Market Tutor, and Market Commons. Availability depends on the member's plan, actual lesson release, and independently verified access rights. A listed Lab does not necessarily mean its assessment or historical example has been reviewed or released.

Free Membership includes a structured beginner pathway and original fictional decision-making practice. Guided instruction introduces the subject before practice; official assessment, completion evidence, and proficiency are kept separate from ungraded reflections. Members can revisit their learning and saved records through their account, subject to published usage limits.

Chart practice and connected market data

Our chart workbench supports an originally authored fictional starter and member-supplied OHLCV CSV files. It also provides integrations for members who connect their own Tiingo market-data account or Alpaca market-data account (IEX coverage), subject to provider availability, account authorization, and the relevant plan or service limits. The Tiingo connection is used for eligible user-authorized price observations; Alpaca IEX observations represent a single exchange and must not be described as the full consolidated U.S. market. Provider data is distinguished from fictional lessons, independently verified historical datasets, and brokerage executions.

The platform also includes a separate optional Alpaca paper-trading connection for eligible account holders. Paper orders are simulated brokerage activity at the provider, not live-money trades and not fills generated from the website's historical playback. No member is required to link a brokerage or market-data account to begin the Free foundational lessons.

Account controls and learning integrity

Member accounts retain their own learning activity, preferences, selected profile avatar, and permitted learning tools. Release availability, premium entitlements, and protected assessment operations are verified on the server rather than granted by selecting an item in the interface. Unreviewed guided beta lessons may support practice but do not automatically award a reviewed grade, competency certification, or Crownlight Sparks.

Our teaching philosophy

Learn it. Understand it.
Practice it. Trade it.

Build knowledge before relying on it. Explain what you see, practice with purpose, and make decisions through a repeatable process. These four principles describe why our books and learning environment work together.

01 · Build the foundation

Learn.

Start with the meaning of a concept, not a signal to copy. We introduce the vocabulary, purpose, mechanics, and limits before asking you to use a skill. No prior market knowledge should be required to begin the Foundations sequence.

How we teach it

Work through prerequisites in order. Learn what each term means, why it matters, how to recognize it, and what it cannot tell you. Books provide a structured explanation; related website lessons and permitted Labs help you examine the idea in context.

A definition is a starting point. For example, learning the words “bid” and “ask” should lead to an explanation of the two sides of a quote and why their difference matters when considering an order.

Your checkpoint: Explain the concept in your own words and identify one limitation. If the explanation depends on repeating a memorized phrase, revisit the lesson.

02 · Reason from evidence

Understand.

Understanding means connecting a definition to evidence and explaining why a conclusion is reasonable. We ask you to distinguish what is directly visible from what you infer, consider another interpretation, and identify missing information.

How we teach it

Observe examples, reproduce the analysis, and explain the relationship between the concept and the available information. Conceptual diagrams explain mechanisms; market-behavior lessons use historical examples where verified evidence is available. Illustrations and simulations must be identified as such.

A chart can show price and volume. It does not directly establish a participant’s hidden intentions. A familiar pattern alone is not a complete decision or a promise of what happens next.

Your checkpoint: State what you observed, what you inferred, and what would weaken your interpretation. Recognizing a shape without explaining its context is not enough.

03 · Turn knowledge into a process

Practice.

Practice is deliberate repetition with feedback. The goal is to make an independent decision using the information available at the time, then review the reasoning. Seeing the answer first and recognizing it afterward is a different task.

How we teach it

Move from guided explanation to your own observations and decisions. Where a Lab supports a frozen decision point, record your interpretation before revealing later information. Use permitted simulation, reflection, and journal tools to document what you did and what to improve.

Review process separately from outcome. A favorable result does not prove the reasoning was sound; an unfavorable result does not automatically show that every part of the process was wrong. Completing an activity is not the same as demonstrating mastery.

Your checkpoint: Repeat the skill with a different example, explain your decision before the reveal, and identify a specific improvement after review.

04 · Apply with discipline

Trade.

“Trade it” describes responsible application of a learned process. It is not an instruction to place a live trade, a graduation promise, or a guarantee of readiness or profitability. Continuing in simulation—or deciding not to trade—is a valid choice.

How we teach it

Before acting, explain the setup, the evidence, the uncertainty, the condition that invalidates the idea, and the risk you are prepared to accept. Review the result against that plan. An educational exercise cannot remove the financial and emotional consequences of a real decision.

Our books, tools, Labs, and community support learning and independent reasoning. They do not provide individualized investment advice or tell you what you must buy or sell. You remain responsible for whether, when, and how to apply what you learn.

Your checkpoint: Explain your plan and limits before an action, including why you might wait or decline. If you cannot explain them, return to understanding and practice.

One philosophy, a detailed teaching sequence.

The four principles summarize the curriculum. Within a lesson, our established sequence is Learn → Understand → Observe → Reproduce → Decide → Practice → Verify. The principles do not replace the Lab stages or change assessment requirements. Return to an earlier step whenever the evidence shows a gap.

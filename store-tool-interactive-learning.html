1. Learn: displayed price is not the same as execution price

The bid is the highest displayed purchase interest; the ask is the lowest displayed selling interest. The difference is the quoted spread. Quotes can move, available quantity can change, and the last trade is not a guaranteed order price.

Fictional security: EXAMPLE Bid: $10.00 Ask: $10.12 Quoted spread: $0.12

Worked situation: A hypothetical learner wants to buy only if the purchase price is no more than $10.05. A market order gives no maximum execution price. A buy limit at $10.05 sets a maximum price but may receive no fill. Neither order guarantees a particular outcome.

At the displayed ask of $10.12, an immediate purchase at $10.05 is not demonstrated. Even if a later trade prints $10.05, that alone does not prove this learner's order was filled: order availability, routing, priority, and size matter. Entering a trade is optional—choosing not to trade may be appropriate when evidence is insufficient.

Common mistake: assuming a limit price guarantees execution, or treating a displayed quote as an executed purchase. This preview deliberately has no broker connection and no future-bar reveal.

The learner wants to buy at $10.05 or below, even if that means not buying. Select the order instruction that expresses that price condition. All quoted prices are fictional.

3. Transfer: recognize when not to assume a fill

Suppose a later fictional price bar touches $10.05 but shows no order book, routing data, or fill confirmation. Write down what evidence is missing before claiming that a buy limit order executed. The responsible conclusion is fill not verified.

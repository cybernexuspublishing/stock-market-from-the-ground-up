A journal that tells the whole story, not just the wins

Fictional, illustrative outcomes — not market history or actual returns. Before calculating any win rate, define which records count and what information is missing. Consider four resolved hypothetical net-dollar outcomes and one unresolved record.

Worked example: calculate without hiding missing outcomes

There are four resolved outcomes, not five. Win rate = 1 ÷ 4 = 25%. The two losses total $80, so average loss magnitude = $80 ÷ 2 = $40. Resolved net outcome = $120 − $50 − $30 + $0 = +$40. Mean net per resolved observation = $40 ÷ 4 = +$10. The fifth outcome is unknown, not a zero or a loss.

Under this specific net-result convention, gain-to-absolute-loss ratio = 120 ÷ (50 + 30) = 1.50. An R-multiple cannot be responsibly calculated without each trade's separately recorded initial modeled risk. The largest peak-to-subsequent-trough change in the ordered resolved outcomes is $80; this is not account-equity drawdown without starting equity and capital flows.

Try it: does selecting only the winner make the strategy successful?

No. Showing the +$120 observation while hiding losses and the unknown result is cherry-picking. Include the full candidate population, missingness, costs, observation period and calculation method before interpreting any metric. A favorable historical or hypothetical example cannot guarantee a future result.

When NOT to calculate: avoid win rate when the outcome classification or denominator is unknown; do not substitute zero for unrecorded fees; do not infer MFE/MAE from a closing outcome without intratrade price observations; and do not confuse process-rule compliance with profitability.
